An Active Revival

Universities, the Tulu Sahitya Academy, and community groups now document Tulu, teach the Tigalari script, and campaign for the language's formal recognition.

Tulu isn't a museum-piece language — it's spoken by an estimated two and a half million people or more along the coast, and in the last few decades it's seen a genuine wave of organized effort to document it, teach it, and push for its wider recognition.

🏫Academic documentation

University departments in the region — including Tulu studies programs at institutions like Mangalore University — research Tulu grammar, dialect variation, and literature, and train the next generation of scholars in the language. This scholarly groundwork underpins nearly everything else in the revival: dictionaries, teaching materials, and script courses all draw on it.

📖 The Karnataka Tulu Sahitya Academy

A government-backed academy dedicated to Tulu literature and culture — publishing books, running programs, and supporting Tulu writers and researchers.

✍️ Tigalari script workshops

Community groups and heritage organizations run classes teaching the historical Tigalari script to interested learners, well beyond the small circle of traditional scribes.

💻 Going digital

The Tulu-Tigalari script's addition to the Unicode Standard opened the door to fonts, keyboards, and searchable digitized manuscripts — infrastructure the revival badly needed.

🎬 Popular culture

A growing Tulu-language film industry, music scene, and social media content keep the language visible and appealing to younger speakers.

📜The push for the Eighth Schedule

A long-running campaign — backed by cultural organizations, elected representatives, and speakers on both sides of the Karnataka–Kerala border — calls for Tulu to be added to the Eighth Schedule of the Indian Constitution, the list of languages given special national recognition and institutional support. The demand has been raised repeatedly in the Karnataka state assembly and in Parliament, and remains one of the most visible causes tied to the broader Tulu revival movement.

🎯

Why recognition mattersEighth Schedule status would open the door to Tulu being used in more official contexts, strengthen its position in education, and — activists argue — help protect it against long-term decline as English and Kannada dominate schooling and administration.

🌱Two traditions, one revival

What makes this moment distinctive is that the revival works on two fronts at once: reviving the historical Tigalari script as a piece of heritage, while the living Paddana oral tradition and everyday Kannada-script writing keep the language actively used. Few minority languages get to draw on both a documented written past and an unbroken spoken present quite this richly.
