Bhoota Kola

One of Tulu Nadu's most mesmerizing traditions — a spirit-possession ritual where a performer channels a deity or ancestral spirit and shares divine messages with the community, in elaborate costume, to rhythmic drumming.

👹 Spirit possession 🥁 Rhythmic drumming 🧬 Hereditary performers 🌙 Performed at local festivals

The tradition

What is Bhoota Kola?

A living ritual, not a staged performance — still practiced across Tulu Nadu today.

Bhoota refers to a spirit or deity, and Kola to the ritual performance itself. Together, Bhoota Kola is a form of spirit worship unique to coastal Karnataka and Kerala, in which a chosen performer — often from a hereditary line of ritual specialists — is believed to become a vessel for a local deity or ancestral spirit during the ceremony.

Villages hold the ritual to seek protection, resolve community disputes, and mark the changing agricultural seasons. It is not treated as theatre by those who take part — for the community watching, the spirit is understood to be genuinely present and speaking through the performer.

Step by step

Inside the ritual

Bhoota Kola unfolds in stages across a single night.

1
Preparation & costume

The performer undergoes hours of elaborate makeup, face paint, and costuming — towering headgear, layered skirts, and ornaments that transform them ahead of the ritual.

2
Invocation & drumming

Hereditary drummers begin a driving, rhythmic beat that builds steadily through the night, calling the spirit toward the performer.

3
Spirit possession

As the drumming intensifies, the performer is believed to enter a trance state and become possessed by the invoked deity or ancestral spirit.

4
Sharing the message

Speaking as the spirit, the performer addresses the gathered community — offering guidance, blessings, or resolutions to local concerns.

5
Community & closing

The ritual closes with collective prayer and offerings, reaffirming the bond between the village and the spirits it honours.

Good to know

Quick facts

🎭

Elaborate costume

Performers wear towering headdresses, painted faces, and layered traditional dress specific to each spirit being invoked.

🥁

Rhythmic drumming

Percussion drives the entire ritual, building intensity from the invocation through to the possession itself.

🧬

Hereditary performers

The role is traditionally passed down within specific families and communities across generations.

🎶

Paddana songs

Sung oral epics called Paddanas are performed during Bhoota Kola, preserving Tulu Nadu's oral literary tradition.

🏘️

A community event

Bhoota Kola is held for and by the village — it is a shared ritual, not a ticketed performance.

🌾

Seasonal timing

Ceremonies are typically held during local festival seasons, often tied to the agricultural calendar.
