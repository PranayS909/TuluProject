# Research resources for the Tulu course roadmap

A working list of websites to pull real content from — vocabulary, grammar, script practice, and cultural material — organized to match the four units in your roadmap (`script.js` → `ROADMAP_UNITS`). Verify anything before publishing it as a lesson; several of these are community blogs rather than academic sources, so treat them as a starting draft, not gospel.

## The two most useful sites overall

**[Easy Tulu](https://www.easytulu.com/)** — by far the best single source for building actual lessons. It's a numbered, structured course (Lesson 1, 2, 3…) that already mirrors what you're trying to build: greetings, numbers, sentence patterns, verb tenses, family words, and more, each as its own short post. A few specific lessons worth grabbing directly:
- [Lesson 41: Useful Phrases](https://www.easytulu.com/2016/12/tulu-lesson-41-useful-phrases-in-tulu.html)
- [Lesson 42: Family Relationships](https://www.easytulu.com/2017/01/learn-tulu-lesson-42-family-relationships.html)
- [Lesson 5: Numbers 1–20 + interrogatives](https://www.easytulu.com/2016/02/tulu-lesson-5-more-interrogative.html)
- [Lesson 3: Simple Present Tense](https://www.easytulu.com/2016/02/tulu-lesson-3-simple-present-tense.html)
- [Lesson 12: Reflexive Verbs](https://www.easytulu.com/2016/04/tulu-lesson-12-reflexive-verbs.html)

**[Bale Tulu Kalpuga](https://yogish.com/)** (yogish.com) — a long-running Tulu-learning blog with its own [Tulu Lessons archive](http://yogish.com/blog/category/tulu/tulu-lessons), a [basic words list](http://yogish.com/blog/tulu/tulu-lessons/tulu-basic-words), and separate posts on traditions like [Bisu Parba](https://yogish.com/blog/tulu/tulu-traditions/bisu-parba) and [Nagamandala](http://yogish.com/blog/tulu/tulu-traditions/nagamandala) that double as cultural-unit source material.

## Unit 3 — Reading Tulu (Kannada Script · Reading Practice · Proverbs · Writing Practice)

- [Omniglot — Tigalari alphabet](https://www.omniglot.com/writing/tigalari.htm) (also useful context for the Unit 4 Tigalari lesson, but the letterform chart is handy for a "what does Tulu look like written" aside)
- Since Unit 3 teaches the **Kannada script** specifically (not Tigalari), any standard Kannada alphabet/learning site works as your base — e.g. general "learn Kannada alphabet" resources — since the lesson is really "read/write Tulu using letters you'd also use for Kannada."
- [Udayavani](https://udayavani.com/) and other [Kannada newspapers](https://www.w3newspapers.com/india/kannada/) — real running text in Kannada script for reading-practice passages; look specifically for their Dakshina Kannada/Udupi regional editions, which sometimes carry Tulu-language content or coverage.
- [Tulu Wikipedia](https://tcy.wikipedia.org/) — a full Wikipedia edition written in Tulu (script: Kannada), launched in 2016. This is genuinely one of the best sources for short, real, graded reading passages. Background: [Wikipedia — Tulu Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tulu_Wikipedia), [Global Voices — Meet the Newly Born Tulu Wikipedia](https://globalvoices.org/2016/08/28/meet-the-newly-born-tulu-wikipedia-the-23rd-in-a-south-asian-language/).

## General-purpose references (useful across every unit)

- **[Easy Tulu](https://www.easytulu.com/)** and **[Bale Tulu Kalpuga](https://yogish.com/)** — your two primary lesson-content sources, listed again because you'll come back to them constantly.
- **[Glosbe Tulu–English dictionary](https://glosbe.com/tcy/en)** and **[TuluVerse](https://tuluverse.in/)** — quick word lookups and translation checks while writing lesson copy.
- **[Männer's 1886 Tulu-English Dictionary (Internet Archive)](https://archive.org/details/tuluenglishengli01mnuoft)** — the historical reference dictionary; slower to use but authoritative and free.
- **[Tulu Wikipedia](https://tcy.wikipedia.org/)** — real native-script reading material at every difficulty level.
- **[Karnataka Tulu Sahitya Academy](https://tuluacademy.karnataka.gov.in/english)** — the official government-backed body for Tulu literature; check their [publications page](https://tuluacademy.karnataka.gov.in/page/Academy+Publications/en) for material you could adapt (with permission/attribution).
- **[Wikipedia — Tulu language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tulu_language)** — solid overview/background, plus a bibliography worth mining for academic sources.
- **A Grammar of the Tulu Language** — an academic reference grammar; a scanned copy is indexed at [dokumen.pub](https://dokumen.pub/a-grammar-of-the-tulu-language.html). Useful if you want lesson grammar explanations to be linguistically accurate rather than just "what sounds right."

### A caution on quality

A lot of this — especially the personal blogs (Easy Tulu, Bale Tulu Kalpuga, TuluBuzz, Raveesh Kumar) — is written by enthusiasts, not linguists, and transliteration/spelling can be inconsistent from site to site. For anything that will ship as an actual lesson (not just background reading), it's worth cross-checking spellings and phrasing against at least two sources, and ideally having a native Tulu speaker sanity-check the vocabulary and example sentences before they go live in the course.
