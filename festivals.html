Festivals

During Dasara and the Tulu New Year, streets fill with dance, music, and spiritual celebration — a yearly showcase of the region's hospitality and strength.

  • Bisu Parba
  • Aati month
  • Dasara & Navaratri

Tulu Nadu keeps its own calendar of festivals — some shared with the rest of Hindu India, others found almost nowhere else. Together they mark the rhythm of the agrarian year, from new beginnings in spring to the demanding heart of the monsoon.

Bisu Parba — the Tulu New Year

Bisu Parba marks the start of the Tulu calendar year, falling around mid-April alongside similar spring new-year festivals celebrated elsewhere in South India. Households mark the day with new clothes, visits to temples, an auspicious "first sight" (bisu kanni) of favorable objects upon waking, and festive family meals — a fresh start woven into the turn of the agricultural calendar.

Aati — the monsoon month

Aati, roughly corresponding to the peak monsoon month, is traditionally considered inauspicious for weddings and new ventures — but it comes with its own distinctive customs. Among the most memorable is Aati Kalenja, a folk performer who visits homes in ritual costume believed to ward off illness and misfortune during the demanding rainy season. Families also traditionally turn to herbal preparations thought to support health through the monsoon.

Dasara & NavaratriThe pan-Indian festival of Dasara (Navaratri) is celebrated widely across coastal Karnataka too, with local temples and communities adding their own regional flavor to the nine nights of celebration.

Kambala season

The post-harvest months bring Kambala race events across the region, often clustered around local temple calendars.

Kola season

The same cooler months are also peak season for Bhoota Kola performances at village shrines.

Celebrated at the table

As with most occasions in Tulu Nadu, festivals are marked as much by food as by ritual — festive meals built around the region's signature dishes are as central to the celebration as the rituals themselves.
