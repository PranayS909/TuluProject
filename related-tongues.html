Two Related Tongues

Tulu is often discussed alongside Kannada and Malayalam, its closest neighbours, yet remains distinct enough to be its own language, not a dialect.

  • South Dravidian family
  • Own grammar & vocabulary
  • Own literature

Tulu belongs to the South Dravidian branch of the Dravidian language family — the same broad grouping that includes Kannada, Malayalam, and Tamil. Geographically wedged between Kannada-speaking territory to the north and Malayalam-speaking Kerala to the south, Tulu has spent centuries in close contact with both.

A Dravidian family tree

Within South Dravidian, linguists generally place Tulu on its own branch — distinct from the Tamil-Kannada-Malayalam grouping, even though all of these languages share a common Dravidian ancestor much further back. In practice, this means Tulu can sound and feel "familiar" to a Kannada or Malayalam speaker in places — shared sounds, some overlapping vocabulary, comparable grammar patterns — without being mutually intelligible with either.

Neighbours, not siblingsCenturies of geographic proximity and cultural exchange with Kannada- and Malayalam-speaking communities explain the family resemblance — it's contact and shared ancestry combined, not evidence that Tulu descends from either language.

A language, not a dialect

Because Tulu has historically lacked the official status Kannada and Malayalam enjoy as state languages, it's sometimes mistakenly assumed to be a dialect of one or the other. Linguistically, that's not accurate. Tulu has its own grammar, its own core vocabulary, and — as covered elsewhere in this course — its own historical writing system in the Tigalari script and its own substantial body of literature, from the Tulu Mahabharato to the living Paddana oral tradition. Status as a "regional" or minority language is a matter of politics and administration, not linguistic classification.

Geographic bridge

Tulu Nadu sits right at the meeting point of Karnataka and Kerala, which is part of why Tulu shows influence from both neighbouring languages.

Shared, then split, scripts

Tigalari and the Malayalam script both trace back to the Grantha script — a linguistic and a scribal kinship at once.

Why this matters for learners

If you already speak some Kannada or Malayalam, you'll likely find a few footholds in Tulu — but don't expect it to behave like either. Treating Tulu as its own system, with its own rules, is the fastest way to actually learn it rather than mentally "translating" from a neighbouring language.

Sources consulted: Wikipedia — South Dravidian languages, Wikipedia — Tulu language. Exact sub-grouping within South Dravidian is debated among linguists — treat this as a general orientation, not a settled taxonomy.
