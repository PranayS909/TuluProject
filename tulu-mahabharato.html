Tulu Mahabharato

Composed by the poet Arunabja around the 15th century, this retelling of the great Sanskrit epic is the oldest substantial work of Tulu literature known to survive.

  • Poet: Arunabja
  • c. 15th century
  • Written in Tigalari

Among the great regional retellings of the Mahabharata that appeared across South Asia in the medieval period, Tulu Nadu has its own: the Tulu Mahabharato, credited to the poet Arunabja and generally dated to around the 15th century. It stands as the oldest substantial piece of Tulu literature known to have survived to the present day — a rare window into what the language looked like some five to six centuries ago.

A regional epic in a coastal tongue

Adapting the Sanskrit Mahabharata into a local language was a common and prestigious literary undertaking across medieval India — Kannada, Telugu, Tamil, and Malayalam all have their own celebrated versions. The Tulu Mahabharato belongs to this same tradition, rendering the epic's story of the Pandavas and Kauravas into Tulu verse for audiences along the southwestern coast. Like other literary Tulu of its era, it was originally written down in the Tigalari script, on palm-leaf manuscripts.

Why scholars careBecause so little early written Tulu survives, the Tulu Mahabharato is an important source for historical linguists — it preserves grammar, vocabulary, and phrasing from a much earlier stage of the language than anything spoken today.

An author still being pieced together

Details about Arunabja's life are thin, and the exact century of composition is debated among scholars who study early Tulu manuscripts — a common situation for regional-language literature that survived only in a handful of hand-copied palm-leaf copies, without the institutional record-keeping that preserved better-known court literatures. What's not in dispute is the text's significance: it's routinely cited as the starting point of documented Tulu literary history.

Palm-leaf survival

Like most Tigalari-script works, it survived in grantha — bundled, oiled palm-leaf manuscripts passed down and periodically recopied.

Studied today

University Tulu departments and literary scholars continue to edit, translate, and publish editions of the text for modern readers.

Written epic, sung epic

The Tulu Mahabharato represents Tulu's written literary tradition — but it wasn't the only way epic stories traveled through Tulu Nadu. Alongside and long before manuscripts like this one, communities preserved their own sung narratives orally, a completely different tradition explored on the Paddana tradition page.

Sources consulted: general references on early Tulu literature including the Wikipedia — Tulu language overview and Tulupedia — Ancient Tulu Literature. Some biographical details about Arunabja and the exact dating remain debated among scholars — treat specifics here as a starting point, not the final word.
